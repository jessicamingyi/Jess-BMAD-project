## Verdict N4

The need is real but thinly evidenced in user voice. There is direct recent evidence of vision-driven choice (1 voice, 2025) plus repeated complaints about legibility: notes size, contrast, dark-mode borders, small indicators (4 voices, mostly old or undated). Mis-tap and mode-confusion complaints exist but are old (2011, 2020). Caregiver voices are basically missing (1 indirect snippet). The supply side is strong: there are several new "for seniors" or "EyeCare" apps, and Brainium has a help-centre FAQ for "numbers too small". That suggests vendors see demand, but it is not user voice. The voices that exist frame readability (large digits, large notes, contrast) as a general need of all players, not something only seniors want. Prevalence is NOT established: the voices come from a single community (App Store), and the RSS feeds for Sudoku.com page 2, Brainium, and PeopleFun (about 150 recent reviews) had zero matches for eyes, font, age, family, or taps, because ad complaints dominate them. Confidence: low-medium.

## Verdict N3

Confirmed in two independent communities: App Store (Brainium reviews) and Hacker News. Both want hints that explain the deduction ("tell you how you could have found that number yourself") rather than reveal a cell. They also want named techniques to come with definitions. Caveat: most HN voices are old (2014–2023). Only one is within the window (2025), and it is about an adjacent puzzle game. The need looks stable over about 10 years, not new. Confidence: medium.

## New needs

- Glossary or definitions attached to technique-named hints, as a step past "explain the hint" (App Store).
- Hints as a springboard to outside study ("go and research new patterns"): link out or a built-in technique library.
- Readability should be read as large *notes/candidates* and *contrast/borders*, not just large digits. Notes legibility is a distinct pain point.
- Accidental-input protection (drag-to-move selection, notes-mode confusion). Old evidence only.
