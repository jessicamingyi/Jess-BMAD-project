# PRD Quality Review — Traveller Sudoku

Calibration: hobby/solo project, deliberately ~2 pages, success metrics omitted on purpose by the user. Judged against that bar. The PRD is still chain-top: §0 says FR IDs exist "so that UX, architecture and stories can refer to them". So done-ness and downstream usability still count.

## Overall verdict
A good hobby PRD. It has a clear, specific thesis ("every solved Puzzle sends you a Postcard from a City"), no filler, and honest scope boundaries. Most FRs are crisp enough to build from. The risk sits in two places. First, the two things that make this game different (Difficulty grading and teaching Hints) are under-defined right where the architect and story writer will need them. Second, the 30-Puzzle content ceiling quietly works against the creator's own test of "whether the creator keeps playing". One User Journey also contradicts the unlock arithmetic.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions are stated as decisions, not hedged: "You can never lose" (§4.1), "mistake highlighting (**off by default**)" (FR-5), "The player chooses the next City" (FR-12), "text only" Postcards (§3). The three Open Questions (§8) are real. Nobody knows yet who writes the Postcard lines, whether a hobby-budget solver can produce good Hints, or whether text-only Postcards will motivate. Q2 correctly marks the main technical risk for the architect. The addendum's "Decisions made while writing the PRD" section gives the reasoning behind each decision, which is more than most hobby PRDs give.

The one gap is that trade-offs are written as wins, never as costs. The addendum also says it is "not part of the PRD", so a reader of the PRD alone does not see even the reasoning.

### Findings
- **[low]** Cost of "Tier = Difficulty" not stated (§3 Tier, FR-12) — Linking distance to Difficulty means a capable solver has to clear 3 Near and 3 Mid Postcards in a Region before any Hard Puzzle, and can never play a Hard Puzzle in a "Near" City. That may be the right call, but the PRD should say what it costs. *Fix:* add one sentence to §7 or FR-12 naming what was given up, e.g. "No free choice of Difficulty: experienced players must progress through Tiers."

## Substance over theater — strong
The Vision (§1) is not generic. It names a personal problem ("a solved grid vanished and nothing changed") and a specific mechanism, and it could not be dropped into another Sudoku PRD. The Calm-by-design constraints (§5) are concrete prohibitions ("no countdown timers, no streak prompts, no flashing effects"), not adjectives. Privacy is concrete ("no data leaves the browser"). The two secondary jobs in §2.1 ("Daily players", "Improvers") each justify a feature: calm play / no game over, and teaching Hints. They earn their place without needing User Journeys. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and the features follow from it: the Postcard journey (§4.3) supplies stakes, and puzzle play, Hints and the calm constraints protect the experience. Leaving out success metrics is stated openly (§7, "the test is whether the creator keeps playing"), and that is acceptable at hobby stakes. The MVP is an experience-type MVP, and the scope logic (text-only Postcards, 5 Techniques, 3 Regions) matches that. The weak spot is that the thesis promises a reason to play *the next* Puzzle, but the content runs out.

### Findings
- **[high]** The 30-Puzzle ceiling undercuts the thesis and the creator's own test (§3 City "Each City has one Puzzle"; FR-13 "30 Cities"; FR-16 Replay; §7) — The game holds 30 Puzzles in total, only 10 of them Hard. Replaying means re-solving a grid you have already solved, with no new Postcard. A daily player will run out in roughly a month, and then "whether the creator keeps playing" measures the content supply rather than the motivation idea. The PRD never raises this. "More Regions" sits in the Out list without being linked to the risk. *Fix:* either add Open Question 4 ("What happens after City 30?") or state the decision outright, e.g. "30 Puzzles is the deliberate test horizon: if I finish all 30 and want more, the thesis holds." Optionally note that FR-1's generator could support more than one Puzzle per City later.

## Done-ness clarity — thin
Many FRs are well bounded: FR-6 ("never end, reset or fail"), FR-7 ("every cell matches the solution"), FR-8 ("one or two plain sentences"), FR-16 ("never creates a duplicate Postcard"), FR-18 ("after the first visit … without a network connection"), and §5 Responsiveness ("under 1 second"). Only FR-1 has an explicit *Consequence*, and that consequence skips the hardest part of FR-1. The Hint FRs, which are the core differentiator and Open Question 2's technical risk, leave the inputs to the Hint engine ambiguous.

### Findings
- **[high]** Difficulty grading is undefined, so FR-1's Difficulty clause cannot be tested (§3 Difficulty "Set by the hardest Technique the Puzzle needs"; §3 Technique; FR-1 "Its Difficulty matches its City's Tier") — No mapping from the 5 Techniques to Easy/Medium/Hard exists anywhere. The FR-1 Consequence only checks that Puzzles are solvable, not that they are at the right Difficulty. The generator and the Tier experience both depend on this. *Fix:* define the bands in §3, e.g. Easy = naked/hidden singles only; Medium = needs a naked pair; Hard = needs pointing or claiming. Add a consequence such as "the test solver's hardest required Technique for every Puzzle falls in its Tier's band".
- **[high]** The Hint engine's inputs and "mistake" are ambiguous (FR-11 "based on the player's current grid and Notes. If the grid contains a mistake…"; FR-5 "mistake highlighting") — Three cases are not covered. (a) "Mistake" is not defined: is it a digit that differs from the solution, or a digit that breaks a row/column/box rule? FR-5 and FR-11 could mean different things. (b) Wrong Notes, e.g. a Note set that has removed the true candidate, are not a grid mistake, yet a Hint "based on … Notes" would reason from false premises. (c) If the player has entered no Notes, the PRD does not say whether pair, pointing and claiming Hints use candidates computed by the engine. *Fix:* add "Mistake" to the Glossary (recommend: a placed digit that differs from the solution). Say that Hints reason from candidates the engine computes from the placed digits, and that wrong Notes are pointed out the same way as wrong digits. Or state explicitly that Notes are ignored.
- **[medium]** Save behaviour across several Puzzles is unspecified (FR-17 "Progress on the current Puzzle"; UJ-2 "Her half-finished Puzzle"; FR-16) — It does not say what happens to a half-finished Puzzle when the player opens a different City, whether the Undo history (FR-4) survives a reload, or whether replaying a collected City starts from a blank grid. The addendum suggests saving each Puzzle separately, but the PRD does not require it. *Fix:* extend FR-17, e.g. "Each City's in-progress state is kept independently. Undo history does not need to survive a reload. Replay starts from the initial grid."
- **[medium]** "Usable on a tablet browser" conflicts with mouse/keyboard-only input (§5 Platform; FR-2 "using mouse or keyboard"; FR-21) — Tablets mostly mean touch, and no FR covers touch input or a digit pad on screen. As written, a tester cannot tell whether the tablet case passes. *Fix:* either add "or touch" to FR-2 with an on-screen digit pad, or narrow §5 to "desktop only; tablet best-effort, not tested".
- **[medium]** FR-20 uses adjectives, not bounds (FR-20 "large and clear, with strong contrast") — This is exactly the "bounds, not adjectives" case. *Fix:* e.g. "text and digits meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast (4.5:1); box borders and highlights meet 3:1 non-text contrast in both themes; Notes stay readable at the minimum supported window width."
- **[low]** Several small FRs leave the end state implicit (FR-9, FR-14, FR-15) — FR-9 "shows the full explanation and the move" does not say whether the move is applied to the grid or only shown. FR-14 "short … reveal" has no duration and no definition of "short". FR-15 "locked" is not defined (presumably "not yet reachable"). *Fix:* add one clause to each (e.g. "the player applies the move themselves"; "locked = not yet Reachable").

## Scope honesty — adequate
This is good overall. Non-Goals (§6) and the Out list (§7) do real work, and seven `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are placed and indexed (§9). Leaving out success metrics is stated, not hidden. The number of open items (3 Open Questions, 7 assumptions, no `[NOTE FOR PM]`) is fine for hobby stakes. The one unacknowledged risk hits the product's central promise.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Album loss from automatic browser storage eviction is not acknowledged (§2.1 "something I own at the end of it"; FR-19; §5 Platform includes Safari; §7 Out "export/import or cloud backup") — FR-19 warns only about the user clearing data. Safari automatically deletes storage that scripts can write after 7 days without a visit for sites not installed as an app. Other browsers can evict storage under storage pressure unless the site has requested persistent storage. With backup deferred, the Album, which is the thing the player "owns", can disappear silently. *Fix:* either add it to Open Questions or as an `[ASSUMPTION]`, or add a requirement: request persistent storage, recommend installing the app (the addendum already plans an installable web app), and widen the FR-19 notice to cover automatic eviction.

## Downstream usability — adequate
There is a Glossary, terms are capitalised and mostly used consistently (Puzzle, City, Tier, Reachable City, Postcard, Album), FR-1 to FR-21 are contiguous and unique, and cross-references resolve (FR-7 → FR-14, Reachable City → FR-12). Both User Journeys have a named protagonist (Jess) and carry their own context. However, one User Journey contradicts the FRs, and some terms that FRs depend on are not in the Glossary.

### Findings
- **[medium]** UJ-1 contradicts the unlock arithmetic in FR-12/FR-13 (UJ-1 "Her Album shows 7 Postcards from Europe. She picks Lisbon … which is a Medium Puzzle … a Postcard from Lisbon quietly lands in her Album") — With 4 Near / 3 Mid / 3 Far and Far unlocking only after 3 Mid, any set of 7 Europe Postcards must include all 3 Mid Cities. So Lisbon (Mid) is already collected, and under FR-16 replaying it "never creates a duplicate Postcard". Story writers will use this journey as an acceptance scenario. *Fix:* change "7" to "5" (4 Near + 1 Mid), or make Lisbon a Far/Hard City.
- **[low]** Undefined terms that FRs rely on (FR-5/FR-11 "mistake"; FR-15 "locked"; FR-7 "solved") — These are used as states but are missing from §3. *Fix:* add short Glossary entries (see the done-ness findings above).

## Shape fit — strong
The right shape for a hobby/solo, chain-top PRD: short, a light set of 3 jobs, only two User Journeys, both with a protagonist and each covering a different area (the journey loop, and resume/offline), an FR capability spec, and no compliance or persona filler. It is neither over-formalised nor thin. No findings.

## Mechanical notes
- §0 says "FR-1 to FR-N". Replace with "FR-1 to FR-21".
- The "Realizes" tags are uneven: §4.1 claims UJ-2, but the parts that actually deliver UJ-2 are §4.4 (FR-17, FR-18), which has no "Realizes" line, and neither does §4.5. Add "Realizes UJ-2" to §4.4.
- Assumptions Index roundtrip: all 7 inline tags are indexed. The §3 index entry adds "mapped one-to-one to the 3 Tiers", which is not tagged inline (the addendum treats Tier = Difficulty as a decision, so drop it from the index or tag it inline). §5 combines two tags into one index line, which is fine.
- Possibly untagged inferences: the MVP Technique set (§3 Technique) and the three Regions (§3 Region) are stated as fact. Tag them if the user did not confirm them directly.
- FR-1 Consequence says "shipped or generated Puzzles", which keeps addendum options (a) and (b) open. That is fine, but once the architect picks one, tighten the wording.
- Required sections are present for the agreed stakes. Leaving out success metrics is intentional and documented (§7).
